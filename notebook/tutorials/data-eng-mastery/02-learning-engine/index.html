Priya’s Tuesday morning

She opens the dashboard: 3 flashcards due, “Liquid Clustering” flagged weak, recommended next: re-read + 5 practice questions, mock exam readiness 61%. No human tutor is awake. Six small functions produced that morning — this part builds each one with you, and you’ll compute every number by hand before trusting it.

You are here: Part 2 of 6 — the study brain. Part 1 mapped the repo; Parts 3–6 serve this math over HTTP, feed it trustworthy content, let agents query it, and prove it running. The naive version serves random questions and calls it practice; what breaks is trust — Priya rehearses what she knows, skips what she fears, and the score lies. What follows is the machinery that makes the order honest.

The contract of a pure function

Every module in backend/app/algorithms/ obeys one contract: same inputs in, same outputs out — no database, no network, no clock surprises. That is what “pure” means, and it buys three things: tests run in milliseconds, results are reproducible for Priya, and you can reason about each module on paper, which is exactly what follows.

The six residents: mastery (how well known), retention (when to review), weakness (what hurts), recommendations (what next), path_finding (in what order), exam_assembly (fair papers). Nine test files pin them — 86 tests, engine modules at 93–100% coverage.

A recommendation is a weighted sum over measurable things. Real code from backend/app/algorithms/mastery.py (verbatim):

Compute Priya’s score with her: 8/10 quiz (0.8), difficulties 5 (0.6), 6/8 practice (0.75), hands-on done (1.0), correct 7 days ago (e⁻⁰·⁵ ≈ 0.61). Score ≈ 0.32 + 0.15 + 0.1125 + 0.10 + 0.061 = 0.744 → “application”. Notice the embedded teaching philosophy: hard things (0.25) outweigh many things (0.15). When you write scoring, the weights are the product philosophy — choose them deliberately, then test every level boundary (this repo does: 11 mastery tests).

Two production gotchas inside that file

Two senior details in the real file, both worth stealing:

  1. The timezone guard. The database returns timezone-aware timestamps; utcnow() is naive. Comparing them crashes — a classic production incident — pre-empted here in a comment and a conversion. Lesson: every timestamp crossing a boundary gets normalized, no exceptions.
  2. The dual scale. Mastery lives 0–1 here but 0–1000 in storage. Two scales for one concept is a standing invitation to mix them up; the defense is tests on both sides of the conversion. Know both before integrating.

When she sees it again (SM-2)

Mental model: each card carries ease (how well known) and interval (when to resurface). Trace Priya’s card with her: quality=4, ease=2.5, interval=6, reps=2 → 15 days, ease 2.5, reps 3. Now quality=2 → tomorrow, ease taxed, reps reset. Two traces and you own the algorithm. Scope note: SM-2 schedules recall (flashcards), never skill — the engine correctly limits it there.

The 1.3 floor matters: without it, repeated failures would drive easiness toward zero and intervals toward nonsense. Every formula needs its guardrails stated — ask of any algorithm you meet: what stops the numbers from going feral?

What’s weak, what’s next, what path

One pattern, three applications — score candidates, rank, act:

Path finding runs Kahn’s topological sort that raises on cycles — a curriculum requiring A-before-B-before-A is broken by construction, and code should prove it. Fair exams come from stratified sampling (40/40/20 across difficulties, seeded shuffle: same seed, same paper). Every threshold is a named constant with a test.

Notice what the weakness formula refuses to do: it never says “study harder.” It names the gap (which topic, what kind of errors, how recent) and suggests the fix. Diagnostics beat exhortations — in software and in studying.

The content behind the math: a real trap

The engine reasons over curated confusions like this verbatim entry from knowledge-base/exam_traps.yaml:

“500 TB table, 3–4 query dimensions, partitioned by date, slow. What now?” → Liquid Clustering on the most-filtered dimensions — with explicit reversals (need DROP PARTITION lifecycle → partitioning wins).

Lesson

Model misconceptions as versioned, tested, searchable data — never as prompt text. Then ask of any knowledge system: where do its exam traps live? If nowhere, it doesn’t understand its domain yet.

The reversal is the tell of honest content: the trap admits when the fashionable answer loses. Content without reversals is marketing; content with them is teaching.

Failure modes of scoring systems

  • Weights nobody chose. Defaults inherited from a tutorial become product philosophy by accident. Defense: every weight has a comment with a reason and a test.
  • Untested boundaries. A score of exactly 0.8 landing in the wrong level mislabels Priya’s readiness. Defense: boundary tests (this repo has 11 for mastery alone).
  • Timezone crashes. Aware vs naive timestamps (Topic 4). Defense: normalize at every boundary.
  • Gaming the metric. Priya re-takes easy quizzes to inflate mastery. Partial defense: difficulty coverage (0.25) outweighs volume (0.15) — the formula anticipates the exploit. No formula survives all gaming; name what yours doesn’t cover.
  • Stale recency. A year-old perfect score still contributes a little. Defense: the 14-day half-life decays it toward zero — check that your decay actually reaches ~0 in your horizon.

Five things to take with you

  1. Weights are product philosophy — write them deliberately, test the boundaries.
  2. Pure functions are debuggable, and debuggable is teachable.
  3. Every formula needs guardrails (floors, timezone normalization, dual scales).
  4. Diagnostics beat exhortations: name the gap, suggest the fix.
  5. Deterministic systems can be checked by hand — that checkability is the feature.
