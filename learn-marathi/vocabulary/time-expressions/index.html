    What you will learn

    Marathi time vocabulary from Units 16 and 18 covering three layers: the clock (तास, मिनिट, वाजले), durations (तास, मिनिट, सकाळ, दुपार, संध्याकाळ, रात्र), and frequency (नेहमी, कधीच, कधीकधी, रोज, अजून). Every entry has Hindi and English meanings, pronunciation and a real example sentence.

    The grammar key: Marathi says the clock "has struck" - वाजले is the verb. साडे पाच वाजले = half past five (literally "five-and-a-half have struck"). Frequency adverbs (नेहमी, कधीच, रोज) sit immediately before the verb — Unit 18 drills this position.

    Tip: साडे + number + वाजले = half past. सवा + number + वाजले = quarter past. डेढ + number + वाजले = hour-and-a-half. The fraction + number + वाजले pattern covers all clock times.

    The clock - वाजले frame

    English "it is five o'clock" → Marathi पाच वाजले (five struck). English "half past five" → साडे पाच वाजले (five-and-a-half struck). The verb वाजले (struck) replaces both "is" and "o'clock". Quarter past: सवा पाच वाजले; hour-and-a-half: डेढ पाच वाजले.

    Day parts & frequency

    सकाळ (morning), दुपार (afternoon), संध्याकाळ (evening), रात्र (night). Frequency: नेहमी (always), रोज (daily), कधीकधी (sometimes), कधीच (never), अजून (still), आधीच (already). Position: adverb immediately before verb — मी रोज खेळतो, never मी खेळतो रोज.

    Common mistakes

    ✗ Saying पाँच बजे for 5 o'clock→✓ पाच वाजले — वाजले replaces बजे

    Marathi uses the verb वाजले (struck); बजे is Hindi.

    ✗ Using काल for tomorrow→✓ उद्या = tomorrow; काल = yesterday

    Hindi कल does both; Marathi splits them. This is the #1 calendar false friend.

    ✗ Placing adverb after verb→✓ Adverb before verb: मी रोज खेळतो (not मी खेळतो रोज)

    Unit 18 pattern — adverbs pre-verb, consistently.

    What is the biggest time trap for Hindi speakers?

    काल means yesterday, not tomorrow. Tomorrow is उद्या. The verb वाजले (struck) replaces 'o'clock'.
